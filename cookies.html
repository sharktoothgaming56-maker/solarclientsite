Cookie Policy

What cookies this website sets, who sets them, what they are for, and how to turn off the ones you do not want.

Last updated September 20, 2026

In short

  • We set no first-party tracking cookies. This site has no accounts, no analytics of our own, and no login.
  • Google AdSense sets cookies to serve, measure, and personalise the advertising that funds the site.
  • You can turn off personalised advertising without losing access to anything here.
  • The SolarClient launcher uses no cookies at all. This policy is about the website only.

What cookies are

A cookie is a small text file a website asks your browser to store and send back on later visits. Similar technologies — local storage, device identifiers, pixel tags — do comparable jobs, and everything in this policy applies to them as well.

Cookies set by the site you are visiting are first-party. Cookies set by another domain whose content is embedded in the page — an ad, for instance — are third-party. Every cookie on this site is third-party.

Cookies we set

None. This website has no user accounts, no login, no contact form, no shopping basket, and no analytics platform of our own. There is nothing for us to remember about you between visits, so we do not ask your browser to remember anything.

The site is static HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It does fetch the current release details from the GitHub API so the download link and file hash stay accurate, but that request sets no cookie and carries nothing identifying about you beyond what any web request necessarily carries.

Cookies Google AdSense sets

This site displays advertising through Google AdSense, which is how hosting and development are paid for. Google and its advertising partners set cookies on your device through those ad units. We do not control which cookies Google sets, and we cannot read them.

Broadly, they fall into these categories:

Google documents the specific cookies it uses in its advertising cookies notice, which is authoritative and kept current.
PurposeWhat it does
Ad serving Decides which advertisement to show in a given slot.
Frequency capping Stops you being shown the same advertisement repeatedly.
Measurement Counts impressions and clicks so advertisers are billed correctly and we are paid correctly.
Personalisation Selects advertising based on your prior activity on this and other sites. This is the category you can switch off.
Fraud prevention Detects invalid traffic and automated clicks.

Google’s own explanation of what it collects through sites that use its services is at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. It is the accurate source for this, and more detailed than we could responsibly summarise.

Consent

If you are visiting from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, a consent message is shown before personalised advertising cookies are set, as required by the ePrivacy Directive and the GDPR. That message is served through Google’s certified consent management platform, and your choice is recorded and respected by Google and its partners.

You can change that choice at any time. Most browsers expose the message again after site data is cleared; Google’s own My Ad Center lets you change your advertising preferences directly and applies across sites.

If you are visiting from California or another jurisdiction with comparable rules, a “do not sell or share my personal information” option is available through the same mechanism.

How to turn off personalised advertising

You have several routes, and none of them cost you access to anything on this site:

  • Google My Ad Center — turn off personalised ads across every Google service and partner site in one place. This is the most direct option.
  • YourAdChoices (US) and Your Online Choices (EU) — industry-wide opt-out tools covering many networks at once.
  • Your browser settings — every major browser can block third-party cookies entirely. Safari and Firefox do so by default.
  • An ad blocker — we would rather you did not, since advertising is what funds this, but nothing on the site is gated behind seeing ads and we are not going to nag you about it.

Turning off personalisation does not remove advertising; it means the advertising you see is chosen without reference to your browsing history.

The launcher does not use cookies

Worth stating separately, because people reasonably assume a policy like this covers the application too.

The SolarClient desktop launcher is not a web browser and sets no cookies. It stores your settings, your Microsoft session token, your waypoints and your HUD layout locally on your own machine, in its configuration directory. It sends no telemetry and reports nothing about your usage to us. The privacy policy covers the launcher in full.

Changes to this policy

If we add a service that sets cookies, this page gets updated and the date at the top changes. We have no plans to add analytics, but if that changes you will be able to read about it here rather than discover it.

Questions

If something here is unclear or you think we have got something wrong, ask in our Discord server. Questions about what Google specifically does with advertising data are best directed to Google, since they are the party that holds it — but we will happily point you at the right documentation.
